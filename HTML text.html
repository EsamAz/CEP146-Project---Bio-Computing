Bio-Computing: The Future of Computing

CEP146 Group Project — Video Script

Presenters: Esam, Murshid, Louie, Liria
Target duration: 2 minutes 30 seconds to 3 minutes

Part 1 — Esam: Introduction

Hello everyone. Today, our group will introduce bio-computing, an emerging technology that brings together computer science, biology, and data engineering.

Traditional computers use silicon-based hardware to process and store information. However, researchers are exploring biological systems as another way to perform computing tasks.

Bio-computing uses biological materials, including cells and biological molecules, to process information.

This technology is still developing, but researchers are investigating how it could create new possibilities for computing.

Now, Murshid will explain how bio-computing works.

Part 2 — Murshid: How Bio-Computing Works

Thank you, Esam.

One interesting example of bio-computing involves engineered bacteria.

Researchers have demonstrated that bacteria can perform simple logical computations using biological processes, including gene circuits and molecular signals.

In traditional computers, electronic circuits process information. In bio-computing, biological systems can respond to signals and perform certain operations.

The research notes also explain that biological computing could support decentralized processing, where computations happen inside biological environments.

Now, Louie will discuss another important development.

Part 3 — Louie: Biological Neural Networks

Thank you, Murshid.

Another interesting development is neuromorphic computing using living cells.

Researchers are exploring ways to combine biological neural networks with computing technology.

These systems use biological pathways to process information and may support adaptive computing.

Unlike traditional silicon-based processors, biological systems can respond dynamically to their environments.

Researchers are also interested in whether these approaches could reduce energy consumption for certain computing tasks.

Now, Liria will conclude our presentation.

Part 4 — Liria: Benefits and Conclusion

Thank you, Louie.

Bio-computing offers exciting possibilities for the future of technology.

Researchers are exploring biological systems for information processing, new computing architectures, and potentially lower-energy operations.

However, this technology is still experimental. More research is needed before it can become a practical alternative to conventional computing systems.

In conclusion, bio-computing shows how biology and computer science can work together to create new approaches to processing information.

Thank you for watching our CEP146 group presentation.
